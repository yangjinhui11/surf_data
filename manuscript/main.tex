\begin{abstract}
Land-surface schemes are mature in process content but are written in
Fortran, confining parameter and sensitivity studies to ensembles of
independent integrations. We present a differentiable PyTorch implementation
of the complete OpenIFS SURF land-surface physics that preserves the tiled
process ordering of the Fortran reference and keeps every conditional regime
switch unsmoothed, so the differentiated operator is the verified one. It
matches the reference at every duration tested: a 30-day controlled
integration of the 97,709-column CMFD domain ends at surface-temperature and
snow-water-equivalent RMSE of $6.90\times10^{-6}$~K and
$1.05\times10^{-6}$~kg~m$^{-2}$; a one-year autonomous integration holds
terminal relative errors below $5.1\times10^{-3}$~\% with the annual water
budget closed to $5.90\times10^{-12}$~kg~m$^{-2}$; and ten successively
restarted years (2009--2018) show no error growth. CUDA-graph execution on
an NVIDIA A100 sustains a 3.8-fold speed-up over the 32-thread Fortran
reference.

The value of differentiability, however, is not speed but closing the loop
between satellite observations and the process formulation, with a control
at every step. Against independent Terra MODIS observations the
configuration reproduces nighttime land surface temperature (bias 0.66~K)
but carries a $-6.4$~K daytime cold bias. Using reverse-mode derivatives
verified against finite differences to $4\times10^{-4}$, we decompose this
bias under the same observations. An observation-space information analysis
identifies the well-conditioned training season (condition number 67 in
autumn--winter versus $2.96\times10^{5}$ in spring--summer), and a
synthetic-twin control under the identical operator and optimizer recovers
imposed parameters to 0.3--1.1~\%; with the inversion machinery so
certified, the same calibration against real observations removes only about
0.5~K of the 5.5--8.3~K daytime offset: a bound on the tested eight-scale
control space, not a general verdict on parameter optimization, which
remains complementary to the structural correction developed next.
Diagnosed from the forcing side, the residual bias projects onto a
ten-control pseudo-forcing profile: an $8.1$~K peak-to-peak deficit of
near-surface diurnal amplitude whose sign changes within the day, a
functional form that no time-constant parameter scale can represent. Read
as a diagnosis of the offline atmosphere--surface exchange rather than as a
correction of the forcing data, the profile guides the design and
calibration of a two-timescale surface-layer closure. State-space verification rejects the freely trained optimum, which
resonates with a dawn-time sensible-heat limit cycle invisible to
quality-controlled observation scores; the adopted configuration rests on
the verified stability boundary and removes 67--84~\% of the daytime bias
(calibration to held-out) in every month of the year. Expressed only in the
surface sensible heat flux and the exchange air temperature, the closure
ports back to the Fortran driver without re-tuning, and a Fortran-guided
retrieval reduces a prescribed 5.0~kg~m$^{-2}$ soil-water bias to
0.170~kg~m$^{-2}$. These results show how a differentiable land surface
moves evaluation beyond numerical fidelity to observation-constrained
process improvement.
\end{abstract}

\section{Discussion}

The controlled boundary-driven integration isolates the numerical translation of the SURF process
sequence from the construction of atmospheric lower-boundary quantities. The
annual autonomous experiment complements that test by exercising the
standalone roughness and surface-layer exchange closure under a realistic,
continuously varying forcing
record. These experiments are complementary: the 30-day case tests the
translated SURF process sequence under fixed boundaries, whereas the annual
case tests
the complete offline pathway. Over a long nonlinear integration, floating-point round-off can be
amplified when an evolving column approaches a thresholded snow, wetness, or
surface-layer regime. The small annual domain-wide errors nevertheless show
that such perturbations remain limited for the evaluated CMFD trajectory.

The offline closure is required only when SURF is driven outside an atmospheric
model. In a coupled OpenIFS application, the roughness and surface-layer
exchange quantities are supplied
through the atmospheric-physics interface, so the translated SURF process
sequence uses the corresponding external-boundary path. This separation makes the reported
verification relevant both to a standalone land-surface workflow and to the
coupled application for which SURF was originally designed.

Several qualifications define the scope of these results. The Fortran--Torch
CMFD experiment tests numerical consistency, whereas the MODIS comparison
provides an independent but configuration-dependent evaluation of surface
temperature and snow cover. The initial state,
static fields, forcing interpolation, and snow configuration are shared by
the two implementations. Agreement therefore demonstrates consistency of the
numerical pathway under those conditions, not uniqueness or physical
optimality of the state. The annual autonomous comparison also
contains the independently reconstructed offline surface-layer closure, so its
small residuals combine round-off effects, threshold activation, and any
closure-level differences. For this reason, the controlled boundary-driven integration is the
primary evidence for the SURF translation, while the autonomous integration
is
evidence for stability of the complete offline workflow. The annual water
budget closes at numerical precision. The reported surface-energy residual is
instead a diagnostic of the staged discrete solve and is not interpreted as a
prognostic conservation residual. The MODIS daytime contrast and snow-cover
underestimation show that numerical agreement does not remove forcing,
initialization, or parameterization error. A longer multi-year protocol should
use seasonally matched land analyses and additional in situ water and energy
flux observations.

The differentiable translation and a learned emulator address the same
interface from opposite directions. The aiLand v1 emulator of the companion
ecLand scheme \citep{Raoult2026aiLand} approximates the IFS land-surface
lineage with a multi-layer perceptron: pre-trained on ecLand offline
integrations, it reproduces the prognostic soil state with a 90-day RMSE of
1.19~K, remains stable over continuous four-year autoregressive
integrations, and is then fine-tuned against FLUXNET flux observations,
reducing latent and sensible heat-flux RMSE by 30 and 20~\% at independent
towers. The derivative of such a surrogate, however, is the derivative of a
one-kelvin approximation of the physics, not of the physics itself, and its
observational gains are absorbed into network weights that map back to
neither physical parameters nor the process formulation. The path taken
here is complementary: derivatives point at the operational physics, so
observation constraints land on physical parameter tables and on the design
of the surface-layer closure, and the resulting correction ports back to
the Fortran reference without re-tuning. Emulators and differentiable
physics translations are natural partners: the former for inexpensive
ensembles and assimilation acceleration, the latter for mechanism
diagnosis, parameter estimation, and structural correction.

The observation-space workflow of Sect.~\ref{sec:modis-evaluation} closes
the loop between diagnosis and process change. The gradient-based
experiments of Appendix~\ref{app:modis-calibration} show that automatic
differentiation supplies efficient derivatives, but experiment design and
seasonal state diversity determine whether those derivatives carry
independent parameter information; the calibration then quantifies the
parameter-explained fraction of the misfit (about $0.5$~K of the
$5.5$--$8.3$~K daytime cold offset) and attributes the remainder to the
missing near-surface diurnal amplitude of the offline exchange, which the
two-timescale surface-layer closure largely supplies. The bound is
deliberately scoped: it covers neither heterogeneous static fields such as
soil-texture, hydraulic, and albedo maps nor the slow soil-water memory
that the anchored ten-day windows suppress, so parameter calibration over a
richer control space remains complementary to, not superseded by, the
closure. The pseudo-forcing profile behind the closure design is likewise a
compensatory projection of the residual onto a forcing-side control space,
not evidence of a several-kelvin error in the station-fused forcing
product; what it establishes is the functional form of the deficit, an
amplitude whose sign changes within the day and which no time-constant
parameter can express, together with the channel through which it acts. The
closure constants are calibrated against one forcing product and one
observation stream, and repeating the diagnosis and calibration under an
independent forcing is the decisive next test. The differentiable model is
therefore most valuable for what it can separate: the parameter-explained
fraction of an observation misfit from its structural remainder, and the
physical shape of that remainder.

The Fortran-guided retrieval (Appendix~\ref{app:differentiability-support})
is the state-estimation counterpart of the parameter workflow: an inverse
calculation whose target is produced by a separately executed reference model
and whose later state is withheld from the update.

\conclusions

The PyTorch implementation reproduces the translated OpenIFS SURF process
sequence in a controlled full-domain boundary-driven integration and is closely aligned with
the autonomous Fortran offline solution through a one-year, 97,709-column
CMFD integration. The equivalence persists over a ten-year successively restarted
integration (2009--2018) without error growth.
Within a pinned software environment, repeated PyTorch integrations are
bitwise reproducible; cross-build round-off differences, amplified by a
decadal offline integration, motivate recording the interpreter, library
builds, and source hashes in every experiment manifest. CUDA-graph execution of the
batched integration reaches 3.8-fold
the 32-thread Fortran reference integration time for the complete
effective CMFD domain, a range consistent with published double-precision
ports of comparable physics \citep{Dahm2023Pace,Lahlou2026CLMml,Lapillonne2026ICON}.
The annual water budget closes with an RMS residual of
$5.90\times10^{-12}$~kg~m$^{-2}$ across the evaluated columns. Automatic
derivatives through a 24~h trajectory agree
with centred finite differences to within $4\times10^{-4}$ relative error for
representative forcing, state, and vegetation controls, with every
conditional regime switch retained unsmoothed: the differentiated operator
is the operator whose equivalence is verified, and the threshold-sensitive
columns where a derivative becomes a local linearization are mapped and
reported rather than hidden. The resulting model
provides a numerically verified and accelerator-ready SURF implementation for
offline process studies; in coupled applications, atmospheric lower-boundary
states are supplied through the established OpenIFS interface.

Independent MODIS evaluation separates this numerical verification from
physical performance. The evaluated offline configuration captures nighttime
LST variability but under-amplifies daytime surface warming and underestimates
snow-cover extent. These diagnosed, spatially structured errors provide
observation-based targets for the differentiable sensitivity and calibration
workflow rather than being attributed to the PyTorch translation. Because the
implementation is differentiable end to end, the daytime cold bias can be
carried past diagnosis to correction: it is decomposed into a
parameter-explained part bounded within the tested control space and a
structural remainder, the functional form of the missing near-surface
process is identified by reverse-mode attribution, and a new surface-layer
closure is calibrated and verified against the same observations. This
closing of the loop between satellite observation and process representation
is the central capability the differentiable formulation adds to the verified
translation.

The gradient-based experiments against the same observations
(Appendix~\ref{app:modis-calibration}) demonstrate end-to-end parameter
training against real satellite data with exact reverse-mode derivatives: an
observation-space information analysis gives condition numbers of
$2.96\times10^{5}$ in spring--summer and 67 in autumn--winter, an
observing-system simulation with the real MODIS operator recovers imposed
table scales to within 0.3--1.1~\% under 1~K observation noise, and the
real-observation calibration reduces day-pass LST RMSE by 5.7~\% in the
calibration window
and 3.8~\% in the seasonally held-out evaluation. Their equally important
output is the bound itself: within the tested space of eight global table
scales, at most about $0.5$~K of the
$5.5$--$8.3$~K daytime cold offset is parameter-explained: a bound on this
control space, not a general verdict on parameter optimization, which over
richer or spatially heterogeneous parameter fields remains complementary to
the closure below. A ten-control pseudo-forcing
attribution shows the remainder corresponds to
roughly 8.1~K peak-to-peak of missing near-surface diurnal amplitude, a
compensatory profile that states the functional form of the deficit without
locating its origin uniquely in the forcing data.

The residual daytime cold offset beyond the calibrated parameter scales is
corrected, in large part, by a process change that complements rather than
replaces a parameter change. The two-timescale finite-capacity surface-layer closure of
Sect.~\ref{sec:structural-slab}, yielding the optimized configuration
SURF-Opt, is derived from the pseudo-forcing
diagnosis, which on its own is a seasonally robust diagnostic profile
rather than a parameterization or a correction of the forcing data, and is calibrated on ten days in two gradient stages: the
coupling coefficient settles at its admissible bound
($\lambda_s = 0.05$~K\,(W\,m$^{-2}$)$^{-1}$), and the day and night
relaxation times are trained with the same machinery. The freely trained
timescales converge outside the integrable domain; a step-level
state-space check shows they resonate with an intrinsic dawn-time
sensible-heat limit cycle in a handful of Tibetan-Plateau columns, an
instability that the quality-controlled observation scores cannot see,
so the training bounds are reset to the state-space-verified domain and
the optimizer settles on its boundary at $\tau_{\mathrm{day}} = 6$~h and
$\tau_{\mathrm{night}} = 3$~h. The calibrated closure removes 67--84~\% of
the day bias (calibration to held-out; day RMSE $11.34$ to $7.17$~K), on
every one of the 20 evaluation days and in every month of the year, at the
cost of a warm-season night warming of about $+1$~K that leaves the night
RMSE essentially unchanged. The closure recurrence is implemented at the
driver level of the Fortran reference and verified to be bitwise invisible
when inactive (Appendix~\ref{app:fortran-slab}); being expressed entirely
in the grid sensible heat flux and the exchange air temperature, it applies
there without re-tuning, so closures designed in the differentiable
implementation are not tied to it.

Using an independent Fortran trajectory, a compact initial-state retrieval
also demonstrates that the verified gradients can reduce a deliberately
introduced land-state error and improve a later held-out state
(Appendix~\ref{app:differentiability-support}). This provides a controlled
foundation for subsequent observation-constrained land-state and
parameter-estimation studies.
